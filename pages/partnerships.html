Brand CSR program

Turn plastic waste and CSR budgets into public good

Our main revenue model: partnerships with brands that have plastic waste — using marketing and CSR budgets to create branded benches and lasting site furniture.

How it works

A simple path from waste stream to waterfront bench.

1

Identify the waste stream

You have plastic packaging, operational scrap, or collected coastal/brand waste. We assess volume, resin types, and logistics together.

2

Align CSR & marketing goals

Define the story: Plastic to Public Good. Decide where furniture will live — campus, park, hotel, Bay-adjacent public space — and how branding appears.

3

Transform into Everwood products

Waste is processed into plastic lumber and fabricated into benches or other agreed products — durable, maintenance-light, outdoor-ready.

4

Install & amplify

Furniture is installed in public or semi-public space. Your brand gains a tangible CSR asset people can see and use — not only a footnote in a report.

5

Measure & renew

Track the metrics we agree on together, such as pounds of plastic diverted and pieces installed, and plan the next wave of product or sites.

Who this is for

  • Consumer and hospitality brands with plastic packaging waste
  • Retailers and CPG companies with CSR / sustainability targets
  • Hotels and resorts seeking distinctive outdoor furniture with a story
  • Institutions that want donor- or sponsor-branded campus seating

Our Chilean partner Desafío Ambiente also offers corporate advisory on eco-design and circular economy around waste (desafioambiente.com).

What you bring

Plastic waste access, CSR/marketing budget, brand guidelines, and preferred install locations in or near Miami / Biscayne Bay.

What we bring

Material transformation know-how, furniture design, outdoor durability, and a clear circular narrative.

What the public gets

Seating and site amenities that resist rot, moisture, and insects — funded by waste that might otherwise be discarded.

An opportunity for the world’s biggest brands

For cities

Florida law limits citywide bans on bags and foam — but cities can still set plastic rules on their own property and in their own contracts, which is where public furniture lives.

Until the Legislature adopts departmental recommendations, local governments “may not enact any rule, regulation, or ordinance regarding use, disposition, sale, prohibition, restriction, or tax of such auxiliary containers, wrappings, or disposable plastic bags.” — Fla. Stat. §403.7033 · flsenate.gov

Polystyrene (foam) regulation is also largely preempted statewide, with an important carve-out: local governments may still restrict polystyrene use by individuals on public property, temporary vendors on public property, or entities in a contractual relationship with the local government (Fla. Stat. §500.90).

Miami Beach

City ordinances ban expanded polystyrene and plastic straws/stirrers citywide, with further rules for beachfront concessionaires, balloons at marine facilities, and consumable plastic décor on public property. Plastic-bag rules for the right-of-way/sidewalk cafés are enforced through agreements rather than a code ban. Source: Miami Beach Rising Above — Regulations.

City of Miami (2026)

The City Commission approved a procurement policy to eliminate single-use plastics, including Styrofoam, in future City contracts with concessionaires on City property — explicitly not a citywide private-business ban, “in compliance with State law.” Source: miami.gov news, March 12, 2026.

Key Biscayne

Effective June 1, 2025, the Village prohibits single-use plastic products in all Village facilities and applies the policy to Village-sponsored events and affiliated vendors — part of “Plastic Free KB.” Source: keybiscayne.fl.gov.

What this means for cities: they can’t always ban bags or foam citywide, but they can control their own beaches, parks, events, and contracts — exactly where Everwood benches and site furniture go.
